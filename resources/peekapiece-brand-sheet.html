Peek-a-Piece

Family photo jigsaws for toddlers. Every piece placed uncovers a little more of someone they love — the app is a peekaboo you play with your own family.

Why the name

Peekaboo is the first game almost every toddler learns, and it works on the same idea as a jigsaw: something is hidden, then revealed. The name says the mechanic and the age group in three syllables, and a two-year-old's grown-up will understand it instantly. No app currently trades under it.

The icon

A jigsaw piece with someone hiding behind it. The eyes do the work — they signal a person, a game, and a face to look for, which is exactly what the app is about. The piece is deliberately one large shape so it survives being shrunk.

Colour

Warm and high-contrast. Toddlers track saturated colour far better than tints, and the four piece colours are chosen to stay distinguishable for the most common forms of colour blindness — they differ in lightness as well as hue.

Build notes

Under-5sPhoto upload

  • Keep photos on the device. If they never leave the phone, you avoid most of the privacy burden entirely — and it becomes a genuine selling point for parents.
  • An app aimed at children has legal obligations that ordinary apps don't (COPPA in the US, the Children's code in the UK, GDPR-K in the EU). Play's Families policy will ask you to declare your target age group. Worth reading before you write the upload feature, not after.
  • No third-party ad SDKs, no analytics that fingerprint, no external links in the child view. Put anything parental behind a simple gate.
  • Design for fat, imprecise fingers: large snap tolerance, no fail state, no timer.
